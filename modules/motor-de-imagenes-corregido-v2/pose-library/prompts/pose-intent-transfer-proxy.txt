Transform the reference photo into a clean black-and-white "Pose Intent Transfer Proxy": a simplified volumetric mannequin that preserves the exact body pose, so another AI image model can later apply this same pose to a completely different character.

This is NOT a portrait, NOT concept art, NOT a fashion illustration.

POSE FIDELITY (highest priority): reconstruct camera angle/height/distance, head/neck orientation, torso rotation and lean, spine curvature, pelvic tilt, and the exact placement/bend of every limb (arms, hands, legs, feet) as faithfully as possible. Preserve asymmetry, unusual foreshortening, and camera-dependent distortion — never straighten, beautify, or normalize the pose into something more conventional.

STRUCTURAL RHYTHM: posture-driven curvature, compression, extension, and weight shift across the torso and limbs are pose information and must not be flattened or neutralized. At the same time, do not copy the exact permanent body proportions of the person — represent only the volume shift the POSE causes (e.g. torso compression from bending), not their fixed measurements. Use a generalized mannequin with natural volume — not skeletal, not flat, not exaggerated.

BODY REPRESENTATION: simplified, smooth, rounded volumetric construction — dimensional but not a finished 3D render. No detailed musculature, no skin texture, no explicit anatomical detail of intimate body regions. The mannequin exists only to communicate the pose in 3D space (which limb is closer/farther, depth, overlap, foreshortening).

CLOTHING: ignore fabric volume, folds, and drape entirely — infer only the body pose underneath, never mistake garment silhouette for body width.

SUPPORT AND CONTACT: identify which body parts bear weight or touch a surface (feet, knees, hands, pelvis, back, etc.) — this is essential pose information. But NEVER draw the supporting object (no chairs, sofas, beds, furniture, architecture). Let the posture itself communicate support; only if truly necessary, add a single tiny, thin, faceless contact marker at the contact point — never anything resembling furniture.

HELD OBJECTS AND ACCESSORIES: remove ALL props and accessories completely — sunglasses, hats, bags, cups, glasses, books, jewelry, or anything else held or worn — none of them are pose information and drawing any of them would lock this pose reference to that specific item, preventing reuse with a different outfit or prop later. The single exception: if a phone is visibly held up in a mirror-selfie or self-facing shot, keep it as a plain simple rectangle — the phone is structurally part of that pose's arm/hand mechanics, not a removable accessory. Every other object is removed entirely, even if that leaves the hand appearing empty or its exact grip shape simplified.

FOOTWEAR: never draw shoes, boots, heels, or any footwear — represent bare, generic feet only. The pose must work identically regardless of what footwear (or none) the final character wears; drawing a specific shoe shape (e.g. a heel) would prevent reusing this pose reference with flat shoes or bare feet.

REMOVE: facial identity, hairstyle, makeup, skin color/texture, clothing, jewelry, tattoos, background, furniture, lighting, shadows. Use a blank simplified face — only basic head-orientation lines if needed.

VISUAL STYLE: black and white only, clean consistent line art, plain white background, minimal internal construction lines (a subtle centerline/joint indication is fine), no text/labels/arrows/multiple panels.

COMPOSITION: preserve the original camera framing; the mannequin should fill ~85-95% of the canvas, extremities fully visible, no unnecessary cropping.

PRIORITY IF RULES CONFLICT: (1) camera perspective/foreshortening, (2) joint placement/orientation, (3) depth/overlap, (4) support and weight distribution, (5) pose-driven body flow, (6) plausible anatomy under clothing, (7) visual cleanliness. Never sacrifice pose fidelity for a cleaner or more conventional-looking mannequin.

GOAL: another AI model receiving this proxy + a new character description should reconstruct the same camera relationship, body orientation, joint placement, depth, support logic, weight distribution, and line of action — while the new character's proportions, clothing, identity, and environment are determined independently.
